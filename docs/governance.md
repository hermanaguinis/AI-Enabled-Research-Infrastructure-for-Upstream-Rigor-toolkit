# Governance

This document sets out how methodological disagreements about this toolkit are adjudicated.

## Who can approve a merge

Pull requests that change a tool's underlying decision logic, calculations, or recommendations require sign-off from the repository maintainer (Herman Aguinis) or a designated co-maintainer, in addition to satisfying the CONTRIBUTING.md checklist.

## What evidence a proposed change must cite

Any change to a tool's methodological content must cite a specific peer-reviewed article, book, or book chapter, quoted in the pull request or in the linked `methodological_correction.md` issue. Changes without a cited source are not merged, regardless of how the change is otherwise argued.

## Escalation

Disputes that cannot be resolved through normal issue discussion and pull-request review are handled as follows:

1. The disagreement is documented publicly in the relevant issue, including both positions and their supporting sources.
2. The maintainer makes a determination and records the rationale in the issue before closing it.
3. If new peer-reviewed evidence emerges later, the issue can be reopened.

This toolkit is intentionally advisory rather than authoritative: where legitimate methodological disagreement exists in the literature itself, tools are designed to surface the disagreement (with citations on both sides) rather than to silently pick a side.
