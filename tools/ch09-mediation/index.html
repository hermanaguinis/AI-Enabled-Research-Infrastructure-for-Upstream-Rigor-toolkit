    2. Theory and Mechanism Builder

    Mediation is a theory of process. Strong mediation claims require a clear account of why X affects M, why M affects Y, and why M transmits the effect of X on Y.

    Mechanism Quality Score

    Complete the prompts to score the mechanism.
    What publication-ready mediation theory should contain

    Completeness: The mechanism is not merely a label for M. It explains the process that connects X to M and M to Y.

    Temporal ordering: The theoretical sequence specifies when X, M, and Y should occur or be measured.

    Rival explanations: The theory acknowledges omitted variables and alternative causal sequences that could produce the same observed association.

    Alignment: The hypothesized model, measurement timing, and planned analysis match the theory.

    5. Measurement Quality Planner

    Measurement error can distort estimates of the paths that form the indirect effect. Use reliable and valid measures, and consider latent-variable SEM when constructs have multiple valid indicators.

    SEM caution: Structural equation modeling is not a cure for poor measurement. It can help address some measurement-error problems when constructs are measured with multiple valid indicators, but reliability and construct validity remain fundamental.

    6. Data and Analysis Planner

    The centerpiece of mediation analysis is the indirect effect, ab. Do not require the total effect c to be statistically significant before testing mediation, and do not treat the direct effect c′ as a condition for mediation.

    Basic single-mediator equations:
    Y = cX + eY
    M = aX + eM
    Y = bM + c′X + e′Y

    Indirect effect = ab
    Total effect decomposition when the direct path is included: c = c′ + ab
    Recommended testing logic
    Estimate a and b, compute ab, and test ab with nonparametric bootstrap confidence intervals. Avoid using the Sobel test as the primary test because the product of coefficients is usually not normally distributed.

    About this tool

    This offline tool helps researchers design, conduct, analyze, interpret, and report rigorous mediation studies. It focuses exclusively on mediation: the mechanism or process through which an antecedent X affects an outcome Y through an intervening variable M.

    It is practical both before data collection and when auditing a completed manuscript before submission. It does not replace expert methodological judgment, a full statistical analysis plan, or field-specific design expertise.
