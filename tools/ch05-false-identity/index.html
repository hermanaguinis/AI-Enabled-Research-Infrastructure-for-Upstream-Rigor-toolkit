Supporting calculators & checks

These helpers feed the wizard's "missed other validity checks" branch. Compute each one, decide which raise a flag, and carry the result back to the wizard. You define which flags count.

① Instructed-response scorer

Instructed-response (attention) items tell the participant exactly how to answer (e.g., "Please select strongly disagree for this item"). The article used 2; targeted participants missed far fewer (mean .41 vs 1.06).

② Within-person reliability

Individual-level consistency. Higher = more reliable. Two methods from the article:

(a) Half-scale (odd/even) correlationSplit each multi-item scale into odd-numbered and even-numbered items, average each half, then correlate the two halves across that person's scales. Strong positive r = consistent responding.

For one participant, list paired half-scale averages — odd-item averages in one box, even-item averages in the other (one number per line, matched order). The tool returns the within-person correlation.

(b) Psychometric antonym correlationCorrelate forward-scored items with their reverse-scored antonyms (e.g., "efficient"/"inefficient") across the person. Reverse-score so that higher = more reliable.

List each forward item's score and its paired antonym's score (one pair per line). Antonyms are reverse-scored automatically, so a higher result means greater consistency.

③ Open-ended completion

Targeted participants voluntarily answered nearly twice as many open-ended questions (2.08 vs 1.11). Low completion is a flag.

④ Time-to-complete flag

Implausibly fast completion suggests careless or non-genuine responding. The article used a < 3 minute rule as one existing check.

⑤ Location cross-check

Compare the participant's self-reported location with what an IP lookup (infobyip.com) returns. A discrepancy is a flag — non-targeted users often mask or misreport location.

Why this matters — the evidence

In a study of nearly 1,000 participants (405 targeted, 581 non-targeted), targeted and non-targeted respondents differed sharply on data quality:

2.5× fewermissed instructed-response items by targeted participants (0.41 vs 1.06; d = .78).
2× higherwithin-person reliability for targeted participants (half-scale .78 vs .37; d = 1.00).
~2× moreopen-ended questions answered by targeted participants (2.08 vs 1.11; d = 1.07).
.66 → .77average scale reliability when using only targeted participants vs. all data; vs non-targeted (.53), error variance dropped over 50%.
|d| = .63average construct-mean difference across 10 substantive constructs; 5 of 9 constructs were not metrically invariant (different factor structure).
Caught what others missedThe IP method flagged false identities that five common cleaning methods (within-person reliability, instructed-response, open-ended, duplicate IP, time-to-complete) left undetected — reducing measurement error by ~25% on average even after those methods were applied.

Substantive conclusions changed too: with targeted-only data, some predictor–outcome relationships flipped sign or changed significance (e.g., LMX → workplace stressors).

Targeted vs. non-targeted — definitions & example ISPs

Targeted participants have low IP threat scores and/or consumer-focused ISPs — genuine, intended members of your population.

Non-targeted participants (false identities) have high IP threat scores and/or proxy-based ISPs — people who took deliberate steps to mask identity/location, and likely lack the characteristics you require.

Consumer-based ISPs (suggest targeted):

ComcastAT&TVerizonTime WarnerCharter SpectrumQwest CommunicationsVirgin MobileUniversities (e.g., Univ. of Washington)

Proxy-based ISPs / hosting / VPN (suggest non-targeted):

Psychz NetworksVultr HoldingsSharktechCyberGhostLeaseweb

Note: legitimate participants increasingly use VPNs (especially since the rise of work-from-home), so a proxy ISP alone is not proof of a false identity — that is why the method combines ISP type, threat score, and other checks before excluding anyone.

Free tools used in the article
  • getipintel.net — enter an IP address, receive an IP threat score (0–1 probability the IP is masked or linked to malicious activity). Public since 2015; updates in real time.
  • infobyip.com — enter an IP address, receive detailed information including the ISP and approximate location. Used to classify consumer vs. proxy.
  • whatismyip.com — reveals the host network/organization and approximate location for an IP. Useful supporting lookup.
  • dnsleaktest.com — reveals information leaked about the host/network, helping detect proxy/VPN use.

These tools' exact computations are proprietary, and other commercial tools may not correlate perfectly. Always report which tool and threshold you used.

Ethical & legal guidance (IRB, GDPR, consent)

IRB balance. Researchers and Institutional Review Boards must balance the validity benefits of collecting and using IP addresses against participants' rights and safety.

EU / GDPR. Authorities in the European Union require formal consent to collect IP addresses. Plan consent language accordingly when any participants may be in the EU.

United States. The U.S. does not require consent to collect IP addresses, but obtaining it is a defensible, wise practice. A hesitation is that asking might deter targeted participants — but it may equally deter non-targeted ones, which is the goal.

Confidentiality vs. anonymity. Because IP addresses can identify a device, consider framing consent forms around confidentiality rather than anonymity (per Teitcher et al., 2015) — a reasonable compromise until governing bodies issue clearer guidance.

Delete IPs when done. Delete IP addresses from all data files after checking for duplicates and completing the screening method, to minimize retention of identifying data.

Glossary of technical terms
  • IP address — unique identifier for a device on a network; recorded by default in most survey platforms.
  • IP threat score — 0–1 probability that an IP is masked and/or tied to prior malicious activity. Near 1 = likely proxy/bot/spammer.
  • ISP — Internet Service Provider; the network the device connects through. Consumer vs. proxy/hosting distinguishes likely targeted vs. non-targeted users.
  • Proxy / VPN — an intermediary server that routes and encrypts traffic, masking the user's true network and location.
  • Within-person reliability — consistency of one individual's responses across items (half-scale and antonym methods). Higher = more reliable.
  • Configural invariance — whether items load on the same factors across groups (same basic factor structure).
  • Metric invariance — whether factor loadings are equal across groups; required to compare relationships fairly. Non-invariance means the same scale "means" different things across groups.
