Interaction Effect Reporting Tool

Report, visualize, and interpret two-way interaction (moderation) effects correctly — operationalizing the three recommendations in Murphy & Aguinis (2022), Journal of Management. Full y-axis by default, an interpretable effect size (ΔR²), interaction type read from slopes (not signs), a region-of-significance “for whom” analysis, and a written statement of meaning.
Why this matters. Murphy & Aguinis reviewed 96 papers across four top journals and found that two-thirds of interaction figures truncated the y-axis (making weak interactions look strong), ~70% reported no interpretable effect size, interaction type was inferred from coefficient signs (which is ambiguous), and no study stated what the interaction means for theory and practice. Interaction effects are typically small in management and related fields — so statistical significance ≠ practical importance. This tool makes the correct report the default.

0 Data input

Preloaded: Sherf & Morrison (2020) self-efficacy (x) × perspective-taking (z) → feedback-seeking (y), on a 7-point scale, as used in the article. A synthetic dataset of N=220 that reproduces the article’s described pattern (a weakening interaction whose prediction error grows at high z) is loaded so every statistic below is internally consistent and recomputed by this tool’s own OLS engine. iExact published coefficients were not reported numerically in the article; these illustrative data reproduce the qualitative pattern of Figures 1–2 so the full workflow is demonstrable.

Enter the unstandardized moderated-regression output: y = b0 + b1·x + b2·z + b3·(x×z).

Fields marked * set the full y-axis and are required to prevent silent truncation.

Paste three columns x, z, y (comma or tab separated, one case per line; an optional header row is auto-detected). This tool runs the OLS regression of y on x, z, and x×z and fills in everything.

IndexValuePlain-language meaning
ΔR²—Proportion of variance in y explained by x×z beyond the first-order effects of x and z. The article’s foregrounded measure.
f² (interaction)—ΔR²/(1−R²full). Cohen benchmarks: .02 small, .15 medium, .35 large.
p (interaction)—Significance of b3. Report it, but never as the sole evidence.
Liu & Yuan (2021)—Variance-of-y-due-to-moderation index; an alternative R²-family measure. Which index you pick matters less than reporting one.

3 Nature of the interaction — read from slopes

Type: —
Do not infer type from the signs of b1/b2/b3. The same type (e.g., strengthening) can arise from many sign combinations (Gardner et al., 2017). This classification is read from the plotted x–y slopes across the range of z.

3 Region of significance — “for whom / over what range”

Johnson–Neyman analysis: the range of the moderator z over which the simple x–y slope is significantly different from zero (α=.05, two-tailed). When effects are small, practical significance is often felt only by cases at or near extreme moderator values.

3 Theoretical & practical meaning (required)

No study in the review did this. Articulate the substantive meaning — for whom and under what conditions the interaction matters, and what decisions it informs (Aguinis et al., 2010).

✓ Reporting-readiness checklist

  • ○Full-range visualization. y-axis spans the entire range of possible y values.
  • ○Multi-value-of-z plot with percentile and prediction-error information (not only ±1 SD).
  • ○Interpretable effect size (ΔR²) reported.
  • ○Coefficient-free classification of interaction type (strengthening / weakening / reversing).
  • ○Region-of-significance / “for whom” analysis completed.
  • ○Written statement of theoretical & practical meaning.
