Orientation

Welcome and orientation

What is a Personal Impact Development Plan?

A PIDP is a structured plan that helps researchers and educators decide where they want to have impact, which competencies they need to strengthen, which activities they will complete, what support they require, and what evidence will show development.

Use this tool as a practical planning workspace: complete the fields that matter now, save your work locally, and revisit it during annual reviews, mentoring meetings, promotion planning, or project launches.

How to use this tool

  1. Complete your researcher profile and impact purpose statement.
  2. Rate six impact competencies using capability, importance, and confidence.
  3. Generate recommended activities and customize your action plan.
  4. Document support needs, evidence of development, and review dates.
  5. Generate, edit, print, copy, or download your PIDP.

Mentoring worksheet

Feedforward interview module

Guided feedforward interview

Use this worksheet with a mentor, department chair, dean, editor, or professional leader.

Describe an event during which you felt you had a very high degree of impact.

What did you do, or not do, that made this impact success story possible? What strengths, capabilities, networks, resources, timing, or conditions contributed?

How can you replicate these conditions in the next week, month, quarter, or year to achieve similar or greater impact?

Interview use

The feedforward interview is designed to identify conditions associated with prior impact success and translate them into future actions, support requests, and review milestones.

Practical tip: Ask the interviewer to help you convert success factors into activities, resources, time frames, and evidence of development.

Context and accountability

Leader, mentor, and institutional support module

Six leader roles

  1. Explain what is required to reach a desired impact level.
  2. Refer the individual to appropriate developmental activities.
  3. Review and suggest improvements to developmental objectives.
  4. Check progress toward objectives and evidence of development.
  5. Remove barriers to progress when possible.
  6. Offer regular check-ins and reinforce positive behaviors.

About this tool

What this tool does

This offline tool helps researchers, educators, doctoral students, postdoctoral researchers, faculty members, and academic leaders create a Personal Impact Development Plan by combining reflection, diagnostic scoring, action planning, support mapping, evidence tracking, and review scheduling.
