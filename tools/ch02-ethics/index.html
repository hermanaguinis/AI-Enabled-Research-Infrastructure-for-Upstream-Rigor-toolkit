1. Project Profile and Ethical Philosophy

Start by defining the study, the people affected by it, and the ethical reasoning that will guide decisions when duties and consequences conflict.

Core study characteristics

Ethical philosophy comparison

Utilitarian lens

Ask whether the expected benefits to participants, science, society, practice, and the public outweigh costs and risks, including the cost of not conducting the study.

Deontological lens

Ask whether the study respects duties and principles such as truthfulness, autonomy, privacy, fidelity, fairness, confidentiality, and respect for persons regardless of likely outcomes.

8. Enforcement, Misconduct Prevention, and Complaint Resolution

Prevent misconduct across planning, recruitment, execution, reporting, and peer review; prepare a proportionate complaint-response process.

Trimming

Selecting, editing, or deleting data to remove inconsistent findings, such as unjustified outlier omission or data dropping.

Cooking

Altering data to support expected results or hypotheses.

Forging

Falsifying data instead of collecting them.

Plagiarism

Using another person’s work or ideas without credit or claiming another’s contribution as one’s own.

Censoring

Withholding, suppressing, or failing to report results because they are inconvenient, contradictory, or sponsor-unfavorable.

Unjustified authorship

Giving or taking authorship credit based on status, power, routine work, or unearned seniority.
