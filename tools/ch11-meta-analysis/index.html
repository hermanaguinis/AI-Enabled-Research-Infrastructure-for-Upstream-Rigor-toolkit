Should this meta-analysis be updated?

The article's Figure 1 three-step decision framework (currency → new methods/studies → probable impact).
Step 1 — Currency: Does the existing review still address a relevant question?
Is the topic still important and actively cited, or has the field, its constructs, or its practical stakes moved on? A still-relevant question is a precondition for updating being worthwhile.
Step 2 — New methods and/or new studies: Is anything materially new available?
Have relevant new meta-analytic methods emerged since (e.g., RVE, PET-PEESE, MASEM, modern outlier diagnostics, Morris weights)? Or has the primary research base grown appreciably, or has new data/IPD been shared?
Step 3 — Probable impact: Could the new methods/studies plausibly change the findings or reduce uncertainty?
There only needs to be a likelihood of impact, not a certainty. Narrowing wide confidence intervals, changing statistical significance, or simply releasing a complete database in an Open Science archive (enabling cumulative growth) all count as impact.

About this tool

An interactive implementation aid. Every checklist item, benchmark, method, and the update wizard are drawn directly from the source article below. The tool captures your choices in-session to help you run a more valid, transparent, and reproducible meta-analysis — and to produce an audit record for authors, reviewers, and readers.
